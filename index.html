Independent AI safety and interpretability researcher in New Delhi, testing whether language models reason or just narrate. Geospatial AI on the side.

Hi, I'm Ayush. I'm an AI researcher and engineer based in New Delhi, currently working independently on AI safety and interpretability. I graduated from Manipal University Jaipur with a B.Tech in Information Technology and a minor in Computational Intelligence.

The question I keep coming back to: when a reasoning model writes out a chain of thought, is that text actually driving the answer, or is it a plausible-looking buffer while the model decodes what it already believed? Questions like that, about whether we can trust what models tell us about their own reasoning and how we would check, are the core of what I care about: AI safety and alignment, interpretability, faithful reasoning, and evaluations that hold up under adversarial pressure. I like looking under the hood.

In parallel I work on geospatial AI: physics-constrained crop-yield forecasting, and national-scale vegetation-stress mapping with an ecologist collaborator at King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM). Before that, at IIIT Delhi, I did geometric deep learning for privacy-preserving fingerprint matching and built lightweight CNNs for spoof detection on edge devices. If any of this overlaps with your work, I'm happy to collaborate, and I like helping people out with what I've picked up along the way.

Off the clock: weightlifting and long walks to clear my head.

##Publications#

Papers still under review are listed with paraphrased titles and no links. Real titles and preprints go up once decisions are out.

  1. under review 2026

    Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness in Distilled Reasoning Models

    venue: A* ML conferencerole: solo author

    Distilled reasoning models copy the look of their RL-trained teachers' chains of thought, but nobody had checked whether that reasoning is load-bearing. I built a token-level protocol that injects adversarial traps mid-generation, inside the model's own thinking, so there's no user-prompt sycophancy to hide behind, and measured whether the final answer follows the corrupted rationale. The short version: as distilled models scale, answers increasingly decouple from rationales (up to ~62% conditional decoupling), with the chain of thought acting more like a compute buffer for decoding latent priors than a faithful causal chain. Solo-author work, from the protocol to the human audit to the GPU bills.

  2. under review 2026

    National-Scale Vegetation Stress and Restoration-Priority Mapping in Saudi Arabia

    venue: Springer Nature journalrole: equal first author

    A postdoctoral ecologist at King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM) brought me the problem: map vegetation stress across all of Saudi Arabia (about 2.15 million km²) and turn it into restoration priorities. I built the ML side, a Google Earth Engine pipeline over 25 years of MODIS imagery that produces vegetation-stress maps and a five-class restoration-priority raster, with XGBoost and LightGBM trained on an 80-feature climate-plus-satellite dataset (CHIRPS, SoilGrids, Sentinel-2 for validation). Spatial-block cross-validation keeps the model from cheating by memorising its neighbours (R² = 0.69), and SHAP points to soil organic carbon as the strongest predictor of vegetation health. We share equal first authorship, and a second paper together is in preparation.

  3. under review 2026

    Physics-Constrained Evolutionary ML for Robust Continental Crop Yield Forecasting

    venue: Elsevier journalrole: first author

    Deep-learning crop-yield models tend to develop what I call belt-centric bias: they memorise the spatial covariates of the best-performing regions and then collapse in climate-stressed marginal ones. This paper forces the model to respect biology instead: econometric spatial fixed effects, evolutionary feature selection that picks physical drivers like soil texture and vapour pressure, and monotonic soil-moisture backcasting to enforce thermodynamic boundaries. Result: predictive parity (R² ≈ 0.80) with heavily parameterised neural networks while stripping about 40% of the data dimensionality, plus SHAP-derived regional phenology maps for free. Embedding physics beats brute-force scaling.

  4. published 2025

    Advanced Crop Yield Prediction Using Machine Learning and Deep Learning: A Comprehensive Review

    venue: TELKOMNIKA, vol. 23 no. 2role: first author

    Before building my own architectures for earth observation I wanted to know everything that had already been tried, so I systematically reviewed 80 papers spanning 2014–2024. Random Forest is the most widely adopted algorithm, but continuous-space deep learning (LSTMs, CNNs) consistently wins on large, multi-modal satellite datasets. The review also catalogues the remote-sensing data everyone uses and the evaluation problems the field still hasn't solved.

    doi:10.12928/telkomnika.v23i2.26621
